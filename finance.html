Computing that keeps money moving

Almost every payment, loan and trade now passes through software. Done well, that makes finance faster, cheaper and safer for everyone who relies on it.

See how

Three ways computing helps

From the trading floor to the card in your pocket.

Algorithmic trading

Programs buy and sell shares in fractions of a second, following rules about price, volume and timing. Because they trade constantly, they narrow the gap between buying and selling prices, which lowers costs for pension funds and ordinary savers.

Exchanges such as the London Stock Exchange run on matching engines that handle millions of orders a day.

Financial modelling

Banks simulate thousands of possible futures, such as a recession or a jump in interest rates, to check they hold enough money to survive them. The same models price mortgages and insurance, and help lenders decide who can afford to borrow.

The Bank of England's stress tests use this approach on the UK's largest banks.

Fraud detection

Machine learning models learn what normal spending looks like for each customer and flag payments that do not fit, often before the money has left the account. They check every card transaction in milliseconds, something no team of people could do.

UK Finance publishes yearly figures on how much fraud banks stop.

Why it matters

A stable financial system protects people's savings, wages and homes. Software lets banks spot risk and crime earlier, and lets new services such as mobile banking reach people who never had a bank account.

There are risks too. Automated trading can amplify sudden crashes, and lending models trained on past data can repeat past unfairness. That is why regulators expect firms to test and explain the systems they use.
